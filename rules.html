Article I: Authority & Mission

Section 1.1 Statutory Authority

Established under the National Aeronautics and Space Act of 1958 (51 U.S.C. § 20101 et seq.), the National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA) operates as an independent agency of the United States Federal Government.

Section 1.2 Core Mandate

  • Expand human knowledge of Earth and space phenomena.
  • Develop, construct, test, and operate aeronautical and space vehicles.
  • Preserve the role of the United States as a leader in aeronautical and space science and technology.

Article II: Governance Structure

Section 2.1 Executive Leadership

The Administrator serves as the chief executive officer of the agency, appointed by the President with the advice and consent of the Senate. The Deputy Administrator assists in agency-wide management and acts in the Administrator's absence.

Section 2.2 Governance Councils

  • Executive Council (EC): Advises the Administrator on strategic direction, policy, and cross-agency priorities.
  • Agency Program Management Council (APMC): Oversees program and project life-cycle reviews and technical readiness.
  • Mission Support Council (MSC): Governs institutional operations, infrastructure, and workforce policies.

Article III: Safety & Mission Assurance

Section 3.1 Independent Technical Authority

Technical Authority resides independently from programmatic chains of command to ensure unbiased engineering, safety, and health decisions during mission design and execution.

Section 3.2 Dissenting Opinion Process

All personnel have the right and obligation to elevate dissenting technical or safety opinions without fear of retaliation until formally resolved by the appropriate Governance Council.

Article IV: Ethics & Compliance

Section 4.1 Standards of Conduct

All employees, contractors, and advisory committee members shall adhere to Federal ethics statutes, avoiding conflicts of interest and safeguarding classified or proprietary data.

Section 4.2 Space Debris & Planetary Protection

Missions must comply with NASA Procedural Requirements (NPR) regarding orbital debris mitigation and biological contamination controls for solar system bodies.

Article V: Amendments & Directives

Section 5.1 Policy Updates

These bylaws and governing directives are reviewed every five years by the Office of the Chief of Staff and may be amended upon formal approval by the Administrator.
